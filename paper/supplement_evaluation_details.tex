\operasubsection{Prespecification}
Every modelling choice was made on the tuning set (patients treated in 2022),
and the test set (patients treated from 2023) was evaluated once, after the
analysis plan had been locked \placeholder{lock date; the plan as a
supplementary file}. Fixed before testing were: the outcomes, horizons and
estimand; the primary and secondary metrics; the paired resampling scheme and
the hierarchical models with their priors; the practical margin (0.01 AUC and
0.01 IPA, one point on the scale of 100 used in the figures) and the labels
better, worse, equivalent and inconclusive, with the paired bootstrap test; the
rules by which the router configuration was chosen on tuning data
(Supplementary Methods, model details); the selection of \opera{}; the
risk-group cut-offs for the comparison with clinical indices; the design of the
borrowing analysis and its candidate pairs, reported descriptively (below); and the sensitivity
analyses (known-status estimand, excluding the 209 patients whose disease was
assigned after day 14, size-matched cut-offs, equal weight per clinical domain,
and the stratum without a dedicated index without nodal marginal-zone lymphoma
and with angioimmunoblastic and Burkitt lymphoma added). The action and
threshold range of the decision curves were fixed before testing: closer
monitoring and supportive care for patients above the threshold, from 5\% to
40\%, with the model's risk-group cutoffs as the two prespecified operating
points.

The router rule was revised once, on 21 October 2026, after rough source-screen
estimates had been seen and before the test set was opened; all four source
banks are compared on the test set, and the rank, query and \opera{} results had
not been seen.
The router screens were then rerun from scratch after an audit showed that the
optimiser's numerical constant (Adam's epsilon, $10^{-6}$) damped the updates of
the routing parameters, whose gradients were mostly smaller; every routing
parameter was retrained with $10^{-10}$ before the router was locked and before
the test set was opened, and the earlier screens are not reported as results.
The rule for the sensitivity fit's starting point (Supplementary Methods, model details) was added on
25 October 2026, from training-time diagnostics of unfinished fits, before
their tuning-panel results or any test result had been seen.

\operasubsection{Weighting of summaries}
Summaries over cells weigh them in one of three ways, each fixed before testing.
Headline differences and \cref{tab:panel-effects} weigh every
disease--outcome--horizon cell equally. Panel summaries and the hierarchical
models weigh every disease equally and, within a disease, every loss endpoint
(the grade variants of one finding together; 52 endpoints) equally; a
sensitivity analysis weighs every clinical domain equally. Router choices and
the borrowing analysis weigh every disease equally, every outcome equally within
a disease and every horizon equally within an outcome. Only estimable cells
enter a summary: at least five cases and five controls, and at least 80\% of
bootstrap replicates with a finite estimate; other cells are shown but not
pooled.

\operasubsection{Borrowing map}
The routed readout adds to the shared patient representation one routed
correction per source disease (routing weight times that disease's adapter
output), and the risk head is linear, so each source's contribution to every
hazard can be switched off exactly while all other weights stay as trained; no weight
is renormalised. A switched-off source's term is replaced by its mean over tuning
patients for the same target disease and outcome (mean ablation); diseases
without tuning patients use the mean over all tuning patients. The router spreads
its weight over many sources, so setting terms to zero would move the
representation far from anything seen in training; the mean keeps the average
shift and removes only what the source contributes for the individual patient.
Zero ablation is reported as a sensitivity analysis. One evaluation of the locked model scored, on the same
patients, the full model, the model without borrowing (the patient's own disease
only), each source alone (own disease plus that source) and each source left
out (all sources but that one). Differences in AUC used one paired patient
bootstrap shared by all variants: the AUC lost without borrowing (full minus
none), the source alone (alone minus none; primary) and the source left out (full
minus left out). The source alone is what a disease adds with no other source,
the source left out what it adds with all the others present. The loss without
borrowing measures what the fitted model uses; it can exceed the gain over a model
trained without borrowing (the own-disease router), because the encoder was
trained alongside the borrowed corrections. Per target disease, source and clinical domain, differences were
averaged over estimable cells (at least five events and five non-events), each
outcome weighing equally and each horizon equally within an outcome. The map
shows the source alone with adaptive shrinkage \citep{stephens2017ash}: within
each clinical domain all pairs share one prior, a mixture of normal
distributions centred on no borrowing with weights fitted to the data, so that
the many near-null pairs are pulled towards zero while large, precisely
estimated ones are kept. Disease groups are not used in the pooling; they
order the map for reading only. Dots mark pairs whose pooled 95\%
interval lies above zero. The AUC lost without borrowing (full against no
borrowing in the fitted router) was estimated per target disease, per outcome
domain and over all diseases, each disease weighing equally
(\cref{fig:borrowing-map}B, D), with the same bootstrap, and set beside routing
(routed against joint model) and borrowing as trained (routed model against the
router trained with its own disease adapter only), and against disease size
(\cref{fig:supp-borrowing-detail}A--B). Candidate pairs (a source disease, a
target disease and a clinical domain) were listed from clinical reasoning and
the literature and kept only if a single-pair estimate could be informative: at
least 200 training patients in the source, so that its correction learned
something to remove, and at least 50 test patients in the target, so that the
estimate was not inconclusive by construction. Smaller diseases remain in the
descriptive map. A confirmatory test of these pairs was planned, with the
expected pairs and negative controls to be rated by study haematologists
without our rationale. Before any rating or test-set evaluation, the routed
model was found on the tuning set to borrow broadly, with similar mixtures of
sources across diseases (correlation of borrowing profiles 0.83 to 0.89), so a
test of a few selected pairs would not describe how it borrows. The pairs are
therefore reported descriptively, each with its unshrunk source-alone gain and
95\% interval (\cref{tab:borrowing-pairs}, with the excluded candidates and the
reason for each), and whether borrowing favours related diseases is described
over all diseases by lineage concentration (main text, Methods). IPA
differences were computed in the same way as a secondary metric.

\operasubsection{Sample size and precision}
The cohort was not sized for this study. It comprises every eligible patient in
Denmark over the study period, so the relevant question is what precision this
fixed sample allows, not how many patients would be needed. Formal criteria for
the sample size of a new prediction model \citep{Riley2020} assume one outcome
and a fixed set of predictors, and do not carry over to a model whose learned
parameters are shared across 69 outcomes. We therefore report, for every
disease--outcome cell, the number of training events and of test events and
non-events (\cref{fig:supp-support}), evaluate a cell only when the test period
has at least five events and five non-events, and judge each estimate by the
width of its interval. For validation, about 100 events and 100 non-events are
commonly recommended for precise estimates of discrimination and calibration
\citep{collins2016validation}. \placeholder{N} cells reached this at one year,
and cells below this threshold are interpreted with particular caution.
Pooling evidence across cells can support a panel-level conclusion but does not
validate a sparsely observed cell for individual clinical use.

\operasubsection{Risk sets and paired comparisons}
The unit of evaluation was one disease--outcome--horizon combination. Its risk
set was determined by eligibility and ascertainment of the outcome, not by the
models compared. For each split we report eligible patients, outcome events,
competing deaths and patients whose status was unknown at the horizon. Within a
cell, every model was evaluated on the same patients and labels. Contrasts can
cover different sets of evaluable cells, so their cell and patient counts are
reported. Cells in which a model could not be fitted or a metric could not be
estimated are shown with the reason (\cref{fig:supp-coverage}) and not treated
as zero effects.

A pooled model could predict cells in which no specialist could be fitted.
These cells have absolute performance estimates but no paired specialist
comparison, so they are reported as added coverage and kept out of the paired
averages. Outcome definitions and the reconstruction of treatment lines are in
the definitions supplement.

\operasubsection{Risk groups, age and operating points}
Continuous model risks were converted to groups fixed at baseline with cutoffs
selected on tuning data, while index groups kept their original definitions.
Kaplan--Meier estimates describe endpoints that include death, and
Aalen--Johansen estimates non-fatal endpoints with death as a competing event.
Group sizes, events and 95\% bootstrap intervals accompany every estimate.
Patients without a recorded index were not included in index comparisons.

In the age figures, patient counts, mean predicted risk and observed risk are
shown as separate rows on the same age bands and groups. The predicted row is
the sequence model's mean probability, also within index categories. The index itself supplies no probability. Estimates are suppressed for cells with fewer
than ten patients, while counts remain visible.

Operating points used thresholds fixed on tuning data, so the specificity
matched on tuning data is not exactly matched in the test period. We report
the achieved sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values
and patient counts. Confusion counts use patients with known status at the
horizon, and the number excluded by censoring is reported. Censoring-weighted quantities are labelled as such.

For the panel as a whole, every model was also evaluated at six decision
points, each fixed per model, cell and horizon on that model's tuning
predictions. The first flagged as many tuning patients as had the event, so
that every model raised the same number of alerts. The second and third
maximised Youden's index and the MCC. The remaining three were the lowest
thresholds with a tuning specificity of at least 80\%, 90\% and 95\%. The test
period then shows how far the achieved specificity drifts from its tuning
target. For each decision point we report the threshold, the confusion matrix,
sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, MCC and the
share of patients flagged, with 95\% intervals from the shared patient
bootstrap and paired differences between \opera{} and XGBoost, the joint
model and the specialist, and the routed and joint models. Confusion
counts below five are suppressed in exported tables, as required by the secure
environment. Cells with fewer than five tuning events or non-events have no
decision point.

\operasubsection{Censoring, calibration and robustness}
The primary metrics weight patients by the inverse probability of remaining
uncensored, estimated separately by disease, outcome and split; an analysis on
patients with known status, without weights, is a prespecified sensitivity
analysis. We report the censoring survival at each horizon and the distribution
of weights, and flag long horizons where little follow-up makes estimates
unstable. The weights are held fixed across bootstrap resamples, so intervals
do not include their uncertainty, and they assume censoring independent of
prognosis, which may not hold where monitoring follows the patient's
condition.

Calibration is reported as calibration-in-the-large, the calibration slope
where estimable, and reliability curves for outcomes chosen by a fixed rule. No
model was recalibrated on test data. Sensitivity analyses by horizon, calendar
year, amount of recorded history and timing of the subtype diagnosis are
reported on their own risk sets. Raw paired effects are reported with the
hierarchical summaries, and the support curve, unsmoothed and binned analyses
are treated as views of the same data rather than independent replications.

Regional performance groups each patient by the region with the most
pre-treatment contacts. The models were trained nationally, so this describes
variation within the development health system rather than validation in
regions excluded from training.

\operasubsection{What the model relies on: attributions and input restriction}
This secondary analysis asks what \opera{} reads in a patient's
history, so that clinicians can judge whether its predictions rest on sensible
information. It is computed once on the model selected on tuning data, before
any attribution is inspected, and every figure and table is a summary of that
one computation. Outcomes, sampling, the reference, the expectations and the
checks were specified beforehand.

\paragraph{Attribution.} Let $f_{k}(E;C)$ be the prediction for outcome $k$ at
one year, where $E$ holds the content embeddings of the recorded events (which
diagnosis, drug or laboratory result and value bin each event was) and $C$ the
context held fixed (each event's time, the age at it, its position and the
patient's subtype). The reference $E^0$ replaces every event's content by the
mean content embedding of training events, an average event, while keeping the
timeline. The encoder was pretrained to predict the next event, so the mask
token was never trained and is not a suitable reference. Integrated gradients
\citep{sundararajan2017ig} move the record step by step from this average
record to the patient's own, and add up how much each event's content changes
the prediction along the way; the integral below is that running sum, and
$\alpha$ runs from the average record ($\alpha=0$) to the patient's record
($\alpha=1$). Event $j$ thus receives the credit
\begin{equation}
 a_{jk}=\sum_d(E_{jd}-E^0_{jd})
 \int_0^1\frac{\partial f_{k}(E^0+\alpha(E-E^0);C)}
 {\partial E_{jd}}\,d\alpha ,
\end{equation}
and the credits add up to $f_k(E;C)-f_k(E^0;C)$. For every outcome $f_k$ is the
log-odds of the predicted cumulative incidence, so that contributions are
comparable across rare and common outcomes and across low- and high-risk
patients. For death, serious infection and severe neutropenia the absolute risk
is also attributed, for the removal checks and the explanations of single
patients. All targets share one forward pass along the
integration path, and the attribution passes through the router, so it
explains the prediction the model actually makes. For death the attributed
quantity is the risk of death, so a positive contribution raises predicted
mortality.

The path integral is evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature with 8 nodes and
repeated with 32 when the absolute gap between the summed credits and
$f_k(E;C)-f_k(E^0;C)$, divided by $\max(|f_k(E;C)-f_k(E^0;C)|,0.001)$, exceeds
0.05. Patient--outcome pairs that still fail are reported by outcome and
excluded from the summaries. Every held-out test patient was explained, and
patient-level outputs stay in the secure environment.

\paragraph{Episodes and concepts.} The unit of interpretation is the episode of
care: events within one day of each other, such as one admission or one
diagnostic work-up, because the diagnoses, results and drugs of one admission
are recorded at different times. Concepts group codes by structure only: ICD-10
to three characters, ATC to the pharmacological subgroup, procedures to four
characters, pathology SNOMED codes to three, and laboratory results to the test
and the third of its value range (low, middle or high) of the latest result.
Codes were named from the official classifications (ICD-10 in its Danish SKS
form, the WHO ATC index, the NPU terminology for laboratory tests, SKS for
procedures and ICD-O morphology for pathology), at the finest level of the
code's hierarchy that had a name, and grouped into the chapters of each
classification and into clinical groups whose diagnosis codes are those of the
outcome definitions (main-text Outcomes section), so a finding and the
outcome it resembles are defined by the same codes. \placeholder{The names of
the concepts shown in the figures were reviewed by [reviewer].}

\paragraph{What makes patients differ.} Because the credits add up, each
patient's prediction is $f_k(E^0;C)+\sum_g c_{g}+r$, with $c_g$ the summed
credit of source (or time window) $g$ and $r$ the numerical residual. The
between-patient variance of the predictions therefore divides exactly into
$\operatorname{cov}(f_k(E^0;C),f_k)+\sum_g\operatorname{cov}(c_g,f_k)
+\operatorname{cov}(r,f_k)$. Each share, a covariance divided by the variance,
is signed and the shares sum to one. The share of the reference is the part of
the differences between patients carried by the timing of care, age and
subtype rather than by what the events were. Intervals come from 1,000
weighted patient-bootstrap resamples.

\paragraph{Outcome-specific use of the history.} For each patient and pair of
outcomes, the episode contributions were ranked within each outcome and
correlated (Spearman). Patient correlations were averaged on the Fisher-$z$
scale with the sampling weights, for patients with at least five episodes and
pairs with at least 20 such patients. A model that had learned only a general
measure of frailty would give high agreement between all outcomes.

\paragraph{Explanations for one patient.} For death, serious infection and
severe neutropenia, explained in absolute risk, each patient's episodes of care
are summed and labelled with their timing and main concepts. Two records are
extracted. For every patient and outcome, the three episodes that most raise and
the three that most lower the predicted risk, each with its contribution in
percentage points, is the format a clinical tool could show beside each
prediction. For one patient, the five episodes that move any of the three risks
most are shown with what each adds for every outcome, so that the reference,
the episodes and the rest of the history add up to the prediction. Concepts are
named from a reviewed list of codes, with laboratory results named by the value
third of the result. Both records stay in the secure environment and pairs that
failed the completeness check are left out. The manuscript shows a synthetic
patient.

\paragraph{Findings and expected effects.} Thirteen findings (seven
laboratory, three diagnostic and three medication findings) are summarised for
every outcome as the mean change in predicted log-odds among the patients who
have them, with 95\% patient-bootstrap intervals, suppressed below 20 patients.
For 21 finding--outcome cells a direction was fixed before the analysis (for
example, high LDH raising predicted death and treatment failure, and low
neutrophils raising predicted severe neutropenia and serious infection). Each is
seen or opposite when the interval excludes zero on the expected or other side,
and unclear otherwise. The other cells are descriptive. Agreement with
expectations shows plausibility, not faithfulness.

\paragraph{Checks.} For faithfulness, the $k$ events with the largest
attributions were replaced by the reference (and, separately, deleted) and the
drop in predicted risk was compared with removing $k$ random events and the $k$
least attributed events, in 200 patients, with $k=5$ prespecified. Deletion can
create unfamiliar inputs, which is why replacement is primary. In 100 patients
the attributions were recomputed with the readout, and with the readout and
encoder layers, redrawn at their original scale \citep{adebayo2018sanity}
(agreement should be low), and with a per-source reference (agreement should be
high). Intervals describe the explanation sample given the fitted model and the
reference, not training-run uncertainty.

\paragraph{Input restriction.} Separately, the same model was evaluated on the
whole test set with restricted input and without retraining: the timing of care
only (every event replaced by the average event, as in the reference), without
laboratory results, without medication, without diagnoses, without the region
and specialty of care (two regions contribute richer hospital-record data, so
region partly marks which data a patient has), and with the last year of
history only.
For each outcome the held-out AUC at one year was compared with the full input,
using paired patient-bootstrap resamples shared across outcomes and
restrictions, and averaged over outcomes and clinical domains. This measures
what the fitted model relies on, not what a model trained on less information
could achieve.

# Main text versus supplement

The current working draft uses Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion.
Earlier Methods-after-Discussion placement followed a results-first editorial
structure; it was not required by the analysis. Use a venue-neutral order for
coauthor review and adapt it to the chosen journal later.

Main Methods must let a reader judge validity without opening supplementary
files: population/index, prospective split and pretraining cutoff, prediction-time
information, eligibility/censoring/death, S/D/O/J/R comparisons, L/E/EA and DAPT,
strong tabular/clinical-score comparators, tuning-only selection, primary metrics,
paired uncertainty and fitted-seed handling. Keep a brief transformer and routing
description; model origin and supervision scope are central interventions.

Supplementary Methods contain exact source code lists, first/second-line
algorithms, outcome thresholds and exceptions; full architecture and optimization
tables; the router equation/gradient ownership/selection details; Bayesian model
formulation, priors and diagnostics; risk-group cutoffs/provenance, feature recipes,
software versions and reproducibility commands.

Main Results carry the headline effect, uncertainty, practical magnitude, harmful
results and key denominator. Supplementary tables retain all cells, horizons,
seeds, coverage, calibration, score populations, costs and sensitivity results.
Do not relegate censoring assumptions, fairness of comparisons, leakage controls
or evidence contradicting the central claim to the supplement.

The long current PDF is a review bundle including supplementary figures and
separately compiled methods. It is not the intended length of the main article.

For the Nature Communications-oriented review on 14 October 2026, the complete
calibration plate is main rather than only a summary. Detailed routing and
portfolio plates are supplementary, with their Results interpretation retained.
This yields nine main figures plus Table 1; all prototype figures remain in the
full synthetic proof. The discussion now uses connected prose without mini-headings.

  conversion, 4 x 32 effective batch 128, 2022-01-01 exclusive cutoff, and

## When the RKKP audit finishes

**Bounded decision (replace open-ended source review):** Run one controlled
V4-versus-V5 MM seed comparison with CLL, treatment, loader cache and code held
fixed. The current 37,547 candidate already uses V5. Separately, run one RKKP
date-only rescue probe: count omitted patients classifiable at their earliest
RKKP date and inventory available registry treatment fields. Inspect only the
critical discordant/missing-treatment groups needed to decide whether RKKP can
provide a credible first-line index and regimen. Then accept the chosen policy,
record limitations and move to the downstream rebuild; do not keep expanding
the date audit unless a release-blocking defect appears.

The controlled V4/V5 comparison is now available: V5 has seven more mapped
patients, but changes 1,033 shared first-line dates; 845 changes exceed 30
days, with V5 later in 924 of the changed cases. The later DaMyDa/clinical-seed
RKKP discrepancies were tested directly with
`opera/R/compare_mm_seed_rkkp_dates.R` against the two mapped candidate CSVs.
Treat registry agreement as supporting evidence, not a gold-standard
adjudication.

**MM seed decision:** Use the frozen V4 line-start table as the primary MM seed
for the final first-line rebuild; retain V5 as a documented sensitivity/source
version, not the primary input. In the direct DaMyDa comparison, among 952
MM-mapped patients whose candidate date changed, V4 was closer to the registry
date for 937 and V5 for 15; V4 was within seven days for 898 versus nine for
V5. Among 771 changes over 30 days, V4 was closer for 757 versus 14 for V5.
This is a decisive consistency check for the intended first-line index, but
DaMyDa and the V4 seed may share upstream provenance, so do not present it as
independent validation. The only remaining first-line policy question in this
bounded review is whether RKKP dates can recover additional patients with
credible disease and treatment evidence. Compare registry-reported treatment
to the pipeline's plan/source, code, indication and episode exclusions; a date
or merely nearby treatment row is insufficient. Then freeze one release and
move on.

The current 37,547-person candidate has 26,745 patients included among those
with a dated entry in at least one of the three RKKP tables. Another 1,800
dated registry patients are absent: 1,299 have no cleaned treatment row, 105
have no eligible row, 199 no pass-1-window row, 137 no qualifying pass-1 episode,
53 no final cohort, six no diagnosis record, and one is excluded after cohort
assignment. These are mutually exclusive *first-failing audit categories*, not
1,800 automatically eligible patients. Among included registry patients, the
absolute index-date difference is 1-7 days for 23,815, 8-30 days for 1,185,
over 30 days for 1,743, and exact for only two. The audit did not record the
**sign** of the difference in its first version. The signed follow-up shows
21,876 of the 26,745 included registry patients have a candidate date exactly
one day *earlier* than their earliest RKKP date. This is a strong systematic
date-representation signal, consistent with but not proof of the previously
observed source-date shift. Exact matches number only two. Of the 1,743
>30-day differences, 1,399 are candidate dates *later* than RKKP; 1,003 of
these are marked `clinical_seed` in the pooled `ANY_RKKP` stratum. The DaMyDa
registry contributes 812 clinical-seed comparisons with a candidate date over
30 days later than RKKP (not additive
with `ANY_RKKP`). Check whether these are genuine different line-one
definitions, V5 seed changes, nonmatching disease seeds, or missing/misaligned
treatment episodes before using the seed as a clinical gold standard. All
counts are INTERNAL, not disclosure-cleared.

1. Confirm the -1-day shift from a small secure-server sample of source
   timestamps and registry dates. Do not apply a blanket correction until the
   provenance is clear. Separately inspect DaMyDa/CLL cases where the candidate
   date is over 30 days later by
   seed date, selected episode date, disease match and treatment-source flag.
   Then review a bounded sample of potentially recoverable cases: no cleaned row, row
   rejected by indication/code/date, diagnosis-window failure, episode
   failure, and index present but no disease classification.
   Distinguish a registry first-line date from evidence of an administered,
   disease-matched treatment. No automatic rescue and no disclosure of small
   exact cells or patient-level rows.
2. Decide one first-line policy: common episode inference for all diseases;
   explicit CLL/MM seed matching and priority; whether any registry-date rescue
   is allowed and what corroboration it requires; cohort classification/forward
   diagnosis grace; CD20 monotherapy and RKKP treatment-row rules. Quantify
   unique patients and index-date changes for each decision before adoption.
   Describe CLL/MM seeds as disease-specific algorithmic line-one estimates,
   not uniformly clinician-adjudicated ground truth.
3. Produce one versioned first-line release with mapped and expanded files from
   the same branch, deterministic options, row/ID assertions, counts and a
   manifest. Preserve all historical candidate files. Freeze the SQL loader
   cache, effective CLL/MM/treatment Parquets, RKKP comparator extracts or
   source definitions/extraction timestamps, upstream collaborator repository
   snapshot, script hashes, package versions and clinical-policy document.
4. Rebuild every index-dependent product from that release: second-line/TTNT,
   observation and follow-up, vetted outcome panel including 30-day TLS variants,
   temporal splits, XGBoost feature matrices from the original server-side
   generator, filtered tabular features and neural fine-tuning inputs. The
   original XGBoost generator path still needs to be found on the server.
5. Run release gates before finetuning: unique patient/index keys; cohort and
   treatment-arm counts; feature times strictly before index; sequence/vocab
   contract and labelled-patient coverage; outcome support/eligibility by
   disease and horizon; split isolation; candidate-vs-prior date/cohort changes;
   no stale v4 paths in resolved configs. Recompute Table 1/CONSORT only from
   the approved release.

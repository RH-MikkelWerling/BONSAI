## Implications

The strongest distinction is the combination: a common treatment decision point; rare and common haematological subtypes; clinically defined outcomes beyond diagnosis-code forecasting; explicit S/D/O/J supervision contrasts; national direct/adapted transfer; and matched tabular and clinical-index evaluation. This is a testable contribution, not a verified claim of priority over all literature.

Avoid novelty claims based solely on many outcomes, competing risks, pretrained model reuse, local adaptation, label efficiency, or individual explanations. All have substantial precedent. The transferable finding must be when each source of experience helps and whether that yields a credible clinical model. Existing papers' cross-system validation and public resources are strengths OPERA should acknowledge rather than imply greater endpoint depth supersedes them.

The journal landscape supports The Lancet Digital Health, npj Digital Medicine and Nature Communications as substantive comparators. Venue and citation totals do not predict acceptance or scientific merit. Older methods and released resources have more time and opportunities to accumulate citations; recent work and split records are especially incomparable.
